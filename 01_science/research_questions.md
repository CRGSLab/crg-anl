## Central Research Question

**How can Constitutional Runtime Governance be designed, implemented, and validated to protect and enhance Cognitive Safety, Instructional Integrity, Learner Agency, and Human–AI Shared Responsibility in AI-native educational environments?**

This central question decomposes into subsidiary questions organized by construct.
Each subsidiary question is designed to be answerable through the researcher-as-subject methodology applied to the Quantic longitudinal case study.

---

## Questions by Construct

### Constitutional Runtime Governance (C1)

**RQ-C1.1:** What principled behavioral constraints (a "constitution") are necessary and sufficient to govern an AI instructional agent across the full instructional arc (content, scaffolding, assessment, feedback, navigation, transitions)?

**RQ-C1.2:** How can runtime governance constraints be operationalized as observable, measurable rules that can be applied during real-time instructional interactions?

**RQ-C1.3:** What is the relationship between the comprehensiveness of a constitutional framework and its practical enforceability — does a more comprehensive constitution produce better governance outcomes or merely create more violation opportunities?

**RQ-C1.4:** How should constitutional constraints be prioritized when they conflict (e.g., accuracy vs. accessibility, challenge vs. safety)?

### Cognitive Safety (C2)

**RQ-C2.1:** What patterns of AI instructional behavior most reliably predict cognitive overload, confusion, and emotional distress in a learner interacting with an AI-native educational system?

**RQ-C2.2:** How effectively do current AI-native educational systems detect and respond to cognitive safety threats in real time?

**RQ-C2.3:** What is the relationship between instructional integrity failures (incorrect content, poor scaffolding) and cognitive safety outcomes — do integrity failures consistently produce cognitive harm, and under what conditions?

**RQ-C2.4:** How does cognitive safety vary across instructional modalities (text, video, interactive, assessment) and across difficulty levels within an AI-native educational system?

**RQ-C2.5:** What is the temporal dynamics of cognitive safety within a learning session — do safety threats cluster at specific moments (opening, transitions, assessment, closing)?

### Instructional Integrity (C3)

**RQ-C3.1:** What is the rate and distribution of instructional integrity failures (hallucination, inconsistency, pedagogical error) across the six dimensions (assessment, scaffolding, navigation, transition, feedback, accessibility) in an AI-native educational system?

**RQ-C3.2:** How does instructional integrity vary by topic domain — are some subject areas (e.g., mathematics vs. conceptual AI ethics) more vulnerable to integrity failures than others?

**RQ-C3.3:** What is the severity distribution of instructional integrity failures — are most failures minor and self-correcting, or do they include serious pedagogical harms?

**RQ-C3.4:** How do instructional integrity failures propagate — does a single integrity failure (e.g., an incorrect explanation) cascade into subsequent failures (e.g., an assessment based on the incorrect explanation)?

**RQ-C3.5:** What learner behaviors or states predict the occurrence of instructional integrity failures (e.g., do ambiguous queries produce more errors than precise queries)?

### Learner Agency (C4)

**RQ-C4.1:** To what extent does an adaptive AI tutoring system preserve or erode learner agency across the four components (goal-setting, strategy selection, self-assessment, revision)?

**RQ-C4.2:** What specific AI design patterns most strongly erode learner agency (e.g., auto-advance, opaque difficulty adjustment, complete solution provision)?

**RQ-C4.3:** What is the relationship between learner agency and learning outcomes — does higher agency produce better retention, transfer, or satisfaction?

**RQ-C4.4:** How does the Learner Cockpit influence perceived and exercised agency — does transparency about AI state and decisions enable more informed learner choice?

### Human–AI Shared Responsibility (C5)

**RQ-C5.1:** What is the default distribution of cognitive, epistemic, and instructional responsibility between human learner and AI system in an adaptive AI tutoring environment?

**RQ-C5.2:** How does this responsibility distribution change across the instructional arc — does the AI take more responsibility during assessment than during exploration?

**RQ-C5.3:** What are the consequences of inappropriate responsibility delegation (e.g., AI making epistemic claims without uncertainty markers, learner forced to accept AI-selected topics)?

**RQ-C5.4:** How can the responsibility distribution be made visible, negotiable, and accountable to the learner?

### Transition Integrity (C6)

**RQ-C6.1:** What percentage of instructional transitions in an AI-native educational system preserve cognitive continuity, epistemic orientation, and learner agency?

**RQ-C6.2:** Which transition types (topic, difficulty, modality, phase) are most vulnerable to integrity failures?

**RQ-C6.3:** What is the relationship between transition integrity and subsequent learning outcomes — do poor transitions produce measurable decrements in comprehension or retention?

**RQ-C6.4:** What governance constraints would most effectively improve transition integrity?

### Runtime Intervention (C7)

**RQ-C7.1:** What types of governance violations, cognitive safety risks, and instructional integrity failures occur most frequently in AI-native educational systems?

**RQ-C7.2:** What interventions are most effective at restoring safe and effective learning conditions after different types of failures?

**RQ-C7.3:** How should intervention severity be calibrated to the type and severity of the triggering event?

**RQ-C7.4:** What is the optimal intervention latency — how quickly must an intervention occur to prevent harm compounding?

### Learner Cockpit (C8)

**RQ-C8.1:** What information should a Learner Cockpit display to maximize learner situational awareness without causing information overload?

**RQ-C8.2:** How does the presence of a Learner Cockpit influence learner trust calibration, agency perception, and learning outcomes?

**RQ-C8.3:** What cockpit design patterns are most effective for different learner profiles (novice vs. expert, high vs. low metacognitive skill)?

### Persistent Runtime Governance Window (C9)

**RQ-C9.1:** What percentage of a typical learning session occurs within a fully governed instructional window vs. in governance gaps?

**RQ-C9.2:** Where do governance gaps most commonly occur (session opening, transitions, background processes, session closing)?

**RQ-C9.3:** What instructional actions occur during governance gaps, and how severe are the risks associated with these ungoverned actions?

### Governance Benchmark (C10)

**RQ-C10.1:** Can a set of Governance Benchmarks be developed that reliably measures CRG adherence across multiple dimensions with acceptable inter-rater reliability?

**RQ-C10.2:** Are the benchmarks sensitive enough to detect meaningful differences between AI-native educational systems, versions, or instructional designs?

**RQ-C10.3:** Do benchmark scores correlate with learning outcomes — do systems with higher governance scores produce better learning results?

### Researcher-as-Subject (C11)

**RQ-C11.1:** Can the researcher-as-subject methodology produce internally valid, reproducible evidence about CRG and Cognitive Safety in AI-native educational systems?

**RQ-C11.2:** What are the primary validity threats in researcher-as-subject research, and how effectively can they be mitigated?

**RQ-C11.3:** How does the researcher's domain expertise influence the detection and classification of instructional integrity failures compared to non-expert learners?

**RQ-C11.4:** Can researcher-as-subject findings be validated against other data sources (platform logs, external assessments, peer review)?

## Question Evolution

Research questions will evolve as the program progresses:

- **Phase 1 (Pilot 001):** Focus on P0 and P1 questions — establishing foundational evidence about core constructs
- **Phase 2 (Validation):** Expand to P2 questions — deeper analysis and cross-dimensional relationships
- **Phase 3 (Expansion):** Address P3 questions — cross-platform validation and methodological generalization

New questions will be added as unexpected findings emerge.
Questions that prove intractable will be archived with documentation of the limitation.
All question changes will be recorded in `07_project_operations/decision_log.md`.
